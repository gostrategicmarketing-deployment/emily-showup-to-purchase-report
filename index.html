The Medicare Coach: from show-up rate to purchases

Webinar attendance, ads, reminders and what Meta sees. Sep 13 to Oct 13, 2026. Prepared Oct 13, 2026.

1. Key findings

  1. Live attendance is the buying path. Of 4,459 recent registrants whose session had already happened, 28 bought. All 28 had attended live. None of the 3,360 who did not attend bought. Attendees bought at 2.55% and produced about $35 of revenue each. Over 60 days the gap holds: 3.00% of 2,166 attendees bought versus 0.07% of 7,299 no-shows (5 buyers).
  2. Show-up rate varies a lot by ad, and it tracks purchases. Show-up ranged from 15.6% to 30.9% across the main ads. Across the 8 ads with enough volume, show-up rate and sales per 1,000 registrants correlate at 0.56 over 30 days and a weaker 0.29 over 60 days across 13 ads (a modest relationship; show-up is not the whole story).
  3. The replay page gets real traffic, but tracking cannot see it, so its effect on sales is unknown. GHL's own site analytics show about 940 views of the Workshop Replay page in the last 30 days. Hyros logged only 12 replay visits from 8 no-shows, and none of those people bought. The replay is not a proven conversion path, but this data cannot rule it out either.
  4. The reminder flow leaves most registrants without a final-hour nudge. Only about a third opt in to texts, and texts are the only channel after the 2-hour email. Registrants who opted in to texts showed up at 35.6% versus 19.0% for those who did not.
  5. No attendance signal is sent to Meta. The one Meta workflow fires a Lead event on form submit. Nothing is sent when someone attends.
  6. Judge ads by cost per attendee, not cost per lead. Medicare Ad Video has the cheapest leads and attendees but the lowest show-up and the fewest sales per registrant (2 sales credited on 534 registrants). Join in 12 Months, Ad Copy 2 has the best show-up and a strong return.
  7. Attribution gaps. Of 119 new sales ($130.6K), 41 had no source in Hyros. GHL recovered 6 of them to ads; 28 ($35K) came through links with no tracking and cannot be recovered.

2. Data and method

Window: Sep 13 to Oct 13, 2026 (the last 30 days), plus 7, 14 and 21 day cuts and a 60-day view (Aug 15 to Oct 13), all ending Oct 13. Sources: Hyros (sales, leads, click journeys, ad-level spend and attribution), GoHighLevel (registrants, the "workshop attendee" tag, messages sent, workflow list), Meta Ads (ad set targeting, spend check, pixel event feed).

  • New sale = any sale that is not the Money Saving Annual Review fee ($297/$397 returning-client reviews). Hyros marks none of these as recurring, so I filtered on the product category and the GHL contact source. Each buyer is counted once; $0 sales are excluded.
  • Attendee = a GHL contact carrying the "workshop attendee" tag. Show-up rate counts only registrants whose session has already happened (sessions run Mondays 6pm CT and Fridays 12pm CT).
  • Registrant = a new GHL contact tagged "workshop registrants" who arrived on a paid-ad link (the ad ID is read from the opt-in URL).
  • ROAS = attributed new-customer revenue divided by Hyros ad spend in the same window (cash revenue, not margin). Spend was cross-checked against Meta on the top ads and agreed to within a few cents.

9. Reminder flow and where people drop off

Reconstructed from the actual messages sent to 240 registrants of the Oct 5 and Oct 9 sessions (120 attendees, 120 no-shows):

  • Right after registering: confirmation email (everyone) and a thank-you text (text opt-ins only).
  • 48 hours before: an email (only for people who registered at least 2 days ahead).
  • The day before: a "starts tomorrow" email.
  • 2 hours before: an email and a text.
  • 30 minutes before: a text.
  • About 20 hours after: a follow-up email; no-shows get "couldn't make it" with the replay.

  • Texts are delivered (about 98%), so the gap is reach, not delivery. Replies to texts are rare (5% to 7%).
  • The last email arrives 2 hours before the session. There is no email at 30 minutes or at start time, so the roughly two thirds of registrants who never opted in to texts get nothing in the final two hours.
  • Session time (6pm versus 12pm) makes no difference to show-up (24.7% versus 24.6%). Registering 4 or more days ahead shows slightly higher show-up (about 31%, small samples).
  • Email-open and click tags may be set after engagement, so part of the open gap could be reverse causation. The text opt-in gap is cleaner because it happens at registration.
  • Three workflows (the pre-workshop 2A, 2B with the 2-hour reminder, and the 30-minute text reminder) were edited on Oct 12 and 13, after the sessions sampled. The flow may have changed since.

11. Recommendations

  1. Send an attendee event to Meta. Build a workflow triggered when the "workshop attendee" tag is added, with a Meta Conversion API action on the same dataset. Use a distinct event (a custom event such as a live-attendee event) so it does not mix with registrations or the checkout Contact event. Turn on custom mapping so matching data is sent, and fire it the same day (Meta rejects old events). About 1,100 attendees a month is enough volume to optimize on, and all 28 recent buyers were attendees.
  2. Judge ads on cost per attendee and attendee-to-buyer rate, not cost per lead. On that basis the Medicare Ad Video ads look worse than their cost per lead suggests, and Join in 12 Months, Ad Copy 2 looks better.
  3. Raise text opt-in. Make the phone number and text consent more prominent on the registration form. Text opt-ins show up at 35.6% versus 19.0%.
  4. Add final-hour email reminders. Send an email at 30 minutes and at start time for people who did not opt in to texts, so they get something after the 2-hour email.
  5. Move the 48-hour email logic to fit when people actually register (median 43 to 48 hours ahead), and consider a morning-of email.
  6. Track the replay. GHL shows about 940 replay views a month but Hyros ties only about 15 of them to a known lead. Put a "watched replay" tag or event on the replay page, and link to it from emails with the contact identified, so replay viewers and any purchases that follow can be measured.
  7. Stop losing attribution. Put the ad ID (h_ad_id) on every paid link and fix the one link where the macro does not fill in. 28 new sales ($35K) came through untracked links.
  8. Review the weak spenders. The May 2025 Video 2 ads return 0.6x to 0.8x on the most spend, and the lowest show-up ads (the Medicare Ad Video ads) have the fewest sales per registrant. Test shifting budget toward Join in 12 Months, Ad Copy 2 and the 65+ Image 1 ad.
  9. Check ad health. Meta flags Join in 12 Months, Ad Copy 2 as having issues; open it in Ads Manager. Consider un-pausing the Join in 12 Months, Ad Copy 1 ad set to confirm its early return on more spend.
  10. Test age. Every ad set targets a single age. Test a wider age range against the current one.
  11. Re-run this report after the reminder changes and the attendee event are live, to see whether show-up and cost per attendee move.

The video family is the strongest at real spend: Ad Copy 2 has the best show-up among the large ads and the best ROAS at scale. What the video does (from the transcript and frames)

  1. The opening filters the audience. "If you're joining Medicare in the next 12 months and you've saved at least $100,000 for retirement." Timing and savings are named in the first six seconds, so people without savings scroll past.
  2. It says the standard advice is not for them. The insurance people and the government advice "are not really for you." The enemy is the advice system, never a person.
  3. A personal story is the proof, with no invented numbers. "My dad and I were quite surprised how many things were not mentioned to him." The post copy extends it: he saved his whole life and still got generic advice.
  4. Both stakes are named. Great health care and protecting hard-earned retirement savings.
  5. The urgency is a real rule, not scarcity. "Do not finalize your Medicare decision until you have joined this session." No deadline and no limited spots.
  6. The ask takes effort. A free live workshop with a five-step process. A considered click, which fits the finding that live attendance is the buying path.
  7. It looks like a real person talking. Vertical selfie, outdoors in daylight, natural expressions, basic captions, no hook bands. Earlier outside-vs-office work found outdoor videos cheaper per search ($3.26 vs $3.82), with sales a tie.

The mechanism above is read from the script, not measured. The ad set is a 10% lookalike of a high-net-worth customer file, so part of the quality comes from the audience. The post copy says "over $500K" while the video says $100,000; that mix is untested. Suggested tests: Ad Copy 2 against its copy on the same audience, and $100K against $500K as the qualifier on the same video.

13. Limits

  • Small buyer counts (28 buyers in the cohort, 2 to 6 sales per ad) make ad-level ROAS directional.
  • Last-click, sale-date window: a sale in the window can come from a lead who registered months earlier, so spend and revenue are not the same cohort.
  • Registrants are new GHL contacts on paid-ad links only; registrants on untracked links are not attributed to an ad (about 6% of tagged registrants).
  • The 60-day view has more repeat payments per buyer and more older contacts re-clicking ads, so sales per ad are credited to whichever ad was clicked last, not necessarily the ad that first brought the person in.
  • Hyros ad-level reports return about 250 ads per call; ads with spend and sales appear in the first page, but a few $0-spend ads with sales may sit on a second page.
  • The household matches are probable, not proven (same last name plus a time zone or IP match).
  • Meta can widen age or placements; targeting here is what the Meta API returned.
